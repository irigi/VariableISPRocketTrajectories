Space Chase

PURSUIT-EVASION IN FREE SPACE WITH VARIABLE-Isp PROPULSION

This interactive applet shows a simple but subtle space pursuit game in gravity-free 2D space. Ship A is trying to reach the base Z and stop there safely. Ship B is trying to prevent that by intercepting A before the mission is complete.

The interesting part is that neither ship is flying with a fixed exhaust setting. Both use constant-power engines with variable specific impulse, following the same propulsion model studied in VariableISPRocketTrajectories. This means that every manoeuvre is a trade-off between how quickly a ship moves and how much of its limited propulsive budget it spends to do so.

The applet first computes A's best route toward the base. It then asks what B can do against it. Depending on the initial conditions, three broad outcomes are possible: A may be able to reach the base before B can stop it, A may be able to avoid capture indefinitely, or A may be doomed in the long run and can only try to delay interception as much as possible.

Boarding mode: B must arrive at the same place and with the same velocity as A, so the interception is a full rendezvous.
Shooting mode: B only needs to pass through A's position, so matching velocity is not required.

Key parameters: power P, wet mass m_wet, dry mass m_dry, initial position, and initial velocity. The available manoeuvre budget of each ship is Δ = 1/m_dry − 1/m_wet.

Use the controls to change the ships, the base position, and the starting state. The trajectory plot shows the paths chosen by the two ships. The fuel-cost plot shows how expensive interception would be for B at different times. Where B's required cost drops below its available budget, interception is feasible.

Based on the constant-power, variable-Isp trajectory model described in VariableISPRocketTrajectories.

The applet runs entirely in the browser — no server required.
